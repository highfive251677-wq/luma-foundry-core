# Premium Template Market Research Notes

## Framer — Official Site

Framer’s public site presents creation as a continuous sequence from idea to launch, framed around the headline **“Framer is the AI design agent for every step from idea to launch.”** Its product demonstration is media-rich: it embeds large canvas previews, CMS views, code-component samples, live-looking controls, and product workflow scenes rather than relying on generic feature lists. It repeatedly pairs capability statements with visible work surfaces and a low-friction acquisition action, **“Get started for free.”** The site also foregrounds CMS, hosting, analytics, localization, collaboration, security, and publishing—signals that a template buyer is purchasing an operating surface, not merely a page design. Source: https://www.framer.com/

## Webflow — Official Site

Webflow frames its offering around outcomes and production control, with an above-the-fold promise of making a website a growth engine. Its public copy emphasizes visual design shipping directly to production, reusable systems, client-manageable CMS content, collaboration, version control, SEO/AEO, analytics, experimentation, and optimization. The page uses feature cards, persona-specific views, UI/video demos, customer-story entrances, and prominent **Start for free** / **Contact Sales** calls to action. For a template seller, the key message is that high-ticket value comes from maintainability and business enablement, not visual appearance alone. Source: https://webflow.com/

## Squarespace — Official Site

Squarespace presents the buyer journey as a guided sequence: start a no-card trial, choose a professionally designed template or use its AI builder, customize layout and brand styling with drag-and-drop controls, add pages/features, publish with a domain and marketing tools, then sell through a secure checkout. Its FAQ stresses templates, pre-built page layouts, portfolios, services, stores, digital downloads, subscriptions, scheduling, invoicing, domains, and social distribution. The public site states that website subscriptions start at **$19/month** after a 14-day trial and that available top-level domains start at **$14 annually**; these are platform plans, not template prices. The practical lesson is to merchandise a template as the first step in a simple launch path, with the buyer seeing customization, publishing, promotion, and selling as one experience. Source: https://www.squarespace.com/

## Lemon Squeezy — Official Site

Lemon Squeezy leads with a concise category promise—selling digital downloads, subscriptions, and software licenses more easily, quickly, and securely—then makes the commerce infrastructure visible. Its official product architecture covers merchant-of-record handling, global tax and payments, payment methods, local currencies, fraud prevention, payment recovery, recurring billing, secure digital-download links, license-key management, hosted or embedded checkout, bundles, coupons, lead magnets, pay-what-you-want pricing, customer management, email, and revenue analytics. The platform states it supports sales from **135+ countries**, local-currency support across **130+ countries**, and **20+ payment methods**, but these claims should be rechecked before operational use. For a template seller, the key takeaway is to package the template with a reliable delivery, licensing, update, bundle, and upsell path rather than treating payment as an afterthought. Source: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/

## Minimalissimo — Official Site

Minimalissimo makes premium perception through editorial restraint. Its landing page removes overt selling mechanics from the primary view and instead leads with a calm, tightly curated visual grid of named design artifacts across products, spaces, and portraits. It keeps item labels short, allows the media to do the work, relies on high-quality art direction, and supports trust through a focused newsletter promise: carefully selected work, ideas, and perspectives that reveal the beauty of less. Its navigation is intentionally limited to Products, Spaces, Portraits, Magazines, About, and Search. For a template business, this implies that a small, highly selective collection can feel more expensive than a broad catalogue if every preview, crop, name, and category is governed by the same point of view. Source: https://minimalissimo.com/

## Playbook — Official Site

Playbook sells the operational result of a visual library rather than storage itself. The homepage opens with **“From upload to final. One library for your entire creative ops.”**, uses product previews at the center of each message, and lets prospective buyers switch between concrete user contexts such as production media, agencies, ecommerce, photography, developers, and social media. It connects benefits to recognizable work states—tagging, versioning, search, AI actions, client handoff, galleries, and brand portals—then uses Start Free and Book a Demo calls to action based on commitment level. Its story-led customer links reinforce that a premium product should show what it becomes inside a buyer’s actual workflow. For templates, replicate this logic with niche-specific preview scenes, a buyer-facing setup guide, editable asset libraries, and a clear handoff experience. Source: https://playbook.com/

## Creative Market — Official Site

Creative Market makes product discovery a merchandising system. Its navigation offers high-level asset categories—Fonts, Templates, Graphics & Illustrations, Brushes & Effects, and more—supported by a broad search field, curated collections, featured shops, staff picks, sale cues, and repeatable product cards. Each card visibly attaches asset image, title, creator/shop identity, category, price, affinity actions, and related-product pathways. The visible example **Poetry Night Vintage Font Duo** is marked at **$25**; this is a single observed product price, not an average for premium UI kits. The marketplace also highlights bundles and licensing resources. A template business can apply this by treating every product tile as a sales unit: show the complete visual outcome, niche, key format, creator quality signal, license summary, price, and a clear next action. Source: https://creativemarket.com/

## Notion — Official Site

Notion uses a broad workspace story to sell templates and its wider ecosystem. Its primary promise, **“Where teams and agents Think together,”** is quickly supported by role-based or outcome-based product paths—capture knowledge, find answers, automate work—and by concrete use-case links such as product-feedback triage, support resolution, security alerts, reporting, and developer tools. Large interface visuals, a live-play control, familiar-company logos, named customer stories, and paired low/high-commitment calls to action (**Get Notion free** / **Request a demo**) lower risk while preserving an enterprise-ready feel. The official footer also exposes Marketplace. The template lesson is to sell not a blank workspace, but a named transformation: a clearly scoped outcome, an immediate trial or duplicate step, and a path to deeper paid help. Source: https://www.notion.com/

## First-Hand Creator Perspective — Framer Template Seller Video

In a June 2025 video titled *Can you really make money selling Framer templates? (my experience)*, an individual creator reported personal revenue and distribution results that should not be generalized to the whole market. The analyzed video quoted the creator as saying that the hero is the most important part of a template for impressing visitors at first glance, that templates in very narrow niches may not have enough actual users, and that Framer Marketplace produced the most effective buyer traffic in their experience. The speaker reported pricing several templates at **$79**, personal year-to-date revenue above **$33,000** at the time of recording, a typical monthly profit range of **$6,000–$8,000**, and **80–90%** of sales coming from Framer Marketplace. They also reported only **$200–$300** per month in the first two months. These are self-reported anecdotes rather than independent market estimates.

The most useful caution was that template sales are not fully passive: the creator said continued work includes design, promotion, customer publishing support, and new-product creation; they also warned that marketplace rankings and competition can reduce revenue. This supports a business model that uses marketplaces for discovery while building a direct audience, documentation, and support system. Source video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aYDR_NWRaIk
